# Brief — 2026-09-10

## OSP news

**[FCC presses "self-regulating" states on whether they actually enforce pole-attachment rules](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-579A1.pdf)** — The Wireline Competition Bureau opened an inquiry into whether the 23 states (plus DC) that certified they regulate pole attachments themselves are actually doing it, and floated stripping that certification where they aren't. If you're pricing or scheduling make-ready in one of those states, the FCC's 90/120/180-day make-ready windows may not apply there at all yet — this proceeding is the mechanism that could force it.

**[NTIA opens a second BEAD funding round, adding months to timelines that use it](https://www.ntia.gov/press-release/2026/ntia-empowers-states-use-bead-savings-location-true-ensuring-universal-broadband-availability-across)** — States can now tap leftover BEAD savings for a "true-up" round covering locations missed due to provider defaults or map changes, but trade outlets estimate it adds nine-plus months where used. Build that slip into any BEAD-linked schedule or bid you're quoting right now.

**[Texas freezes its $13B BEAD program amid a Starlink-favoritism audit](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/12/texas-broadband-office-pauses-funds-disbursement/)** — Lawmakers allege the state broadband office front-loaded payments to Starlink, and disbursement is paused pending a comptroller audit still unresolved as of early September. Treat any awarded-but-unpaid Texas BEAD work as schedule risk, not a committed start date.

**[IEEE 802.3dk standardizes 100 Gb/s Ethernet over a single fiber strand](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.3dk/11116/)** — The new amendment runs full-duplex 100G over one single-mode strand instead of a transmit/receive pair, at 10/20/40 km reaches. Worth flagging to whoever sizes fiber counts on new builds, since it halves the strands needed per point-to-point 100G circuit.

**[Washington's BEAD grant winners are already reporting cost overruns tied to an AI-driven fiber squeeze](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/washington-state-moving-along-on-bead-grants/)** — The state's broadband director confirmed grant winners are seeing real cost increases, blaming AI data-center buildouts for pulling fiber cable and components into "sky-high demand"; Minnesota is separately requesting similar cost-relief waivers. Pad material lead times and unit-cost estimates on any bid you're pricing now — cable cost and availability are moving targets, not stable inputs.

**[Electric co-ops call new federal pole-attachment rules "fatally flawed" as BEAD reopens funding](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/fba-benton-pleased-by-new-bead-round-electric-co-ops-want-course-correction/)** — NRECA, representing 900 electric co-ops, is objecting to January's federal rules that would force co-op poles into federal make-ready terms in any state taking BEAD money, even where state rules don't currently cover co-ops. If you're negotiating attachments on co-op poles in a BEAD project, treat the make-ready terms and cost-sharing as unsettled, not locked in.

## Professional development

**[ISO 9001's sixth edition publishes September 16, three-year transition clock starts](https://committee.iso.org/sites/tc176sc2/home/news/content-left-area/news-and-updates/news-1.html)** — The FDIS passed with strong support and the revised standard replaces the 2015 edition in six days, with transition running to roughly September 2029. If your QMS or QC documentation still references ISO 9001:2015, mark the 2029 deadline now — owning that compliance calendar is a good case for team-lead responsibility.

**[PMI's overhauled PMP exam is already live, weighted toward business environment and AI-enabled delivery](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/project-management-pmp/new-exam)** — The new exam replaced the old one at all test centers July 9, cutting Process from 50% to 41% of content while tripling Business Environment from 8% to 26%. If PMP is on your study list, budget prep time around governance and value-realization topics, not just process mechanics.

**[ASQ's Certified Quality Auditor exam now tests the 2026 Body of Knowledge](https://www.asq.org/cert/quality-auditor)** — The revised CQA BoK became the examined standard starting with the April 2026 window, replacing the 2019 version. Confirm any CQA study materials you're using reference 2026, not 2019 — old prep will miss tested content.

**[Esri's 2026 Utility Network release reworks subnetwork tracing and admin tools](https://www.esri.com/arcgis-blog/products/utility-network/electric-gas/utility-network-2026)** — Delivered via ArcGIS Pro 3.7 and Enterprise 12.1, the release redesigns the Find Subnetworks pane and improves performance for complex network models. If your shop manages fiber plant in a Utility Network model, test the upgrade before rolling it into production design review.

**[ASQ's application deadline for the October certification exam window is September 14](https://www.asq.org/cert/dates)** — The cutoff to apply for the October 1–31 computer-based testing window covers CQA, CQE, CSSGB, and several other credentials; miss it and the next window is December. If CQA or CSSGB is on your list this year, this is the week to confirm reimbursement paperwork and submit.

**[BICSI publishes G3-2026, a new standard for underground OSP pathways](https://shop.bicsi.org/standards/osp-construction)** — The newly numbered standard, "ICT Outside Plant Construction and Installation: Underground Facilities and Pathways," is now live in BICSI's catalog alongside the older G1 and G2.1 series. It's the document a QC reviewer should be citing when signing off on conduit and pathway installs, so worth the read before your next underground punch-list review.

**[FOA reissues its CFOS/D reference guide with expanded project-management chapters](https://www.thefoa.org/FOArgND.html)** — The study text for the CFOS/D design-and-project-management specialist credential added new chapters on project planning, documentation, and managing installation. CFOS/D is the FOA credential that pairs most directly with a move from designer to project lead, so it's the current study text to budget for if you're adding that specialty.

**[QGIS 4.0 migrates to Qt6, which can break older plugins](https://blog.qgis.org/2026/03/09/qgis-4-0-norrkoping-is-released/)** — The first 4.x release moves the underlying toolkit from Qt5 to Qt6, and the legacy 2.x-era Processing API is no longer guaranteed support. If your route or attribute-validation QC workflow depends on older custom plugins, test them against 4.0 before upgrading your production machine, or hold on the 3.40 LTR.

**[BLS updates its construction managers wage and outlook data](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/management/construction-managers.htm)** — The federal handbook now shows a $114,990 median annual wage and projects 9% employment growth through 2035, well above average. It's not telecom-specific, but it's a defensible, citable number to bring into a comp conversation when arguing for a lead or PM-track raise.

## Quality practice

**[Cisco's landmark peer-review study puts hard numbers on why fast reviews miss defects](https://smartbear.com/learn/code-review/best-practices-for-peer-code-review/)** — Analyzing roughly 2,500 real reviews, the study found defect-detection effectiveness dropped sharply past about 400–500 lines/hour or 60–90 minute sessions, regardless of skill. Translate that pace into pages or drawings per hour to set defensible limits for a design review team, and to push back when "move faster" pressure amounts to rubber-stamping.

**[A clean template for writing nonconformities that hold up](https://www.theauditoronline.com/writing-nonconformities/)** — The three-part structure — state the requirement, state the objective evidence, state the conclusion — turns a vague finding into one an auditee can't argue with. The same discipline sharpens ordinary QC review comments on design packages, not just formal ISO 9001 audit findings.

**[CASRAI's CAPA entry defines what "verified effective" actually requires](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/capa-corrective-and-preventive-action)** — Using a worked example, it separates correction, corrective action, preventive action, and effectiveness verification, and is explicit that verification needs a defined metric, a baseline, and a later check. A template for writing a CAPA on a recurring design-package error that survives a "did you fix the person or the system" challenge.

**[NIST's Shewhart chart guidance settles the subgroup-size question with real numbers](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc321.htm)** — The e-Handbook shows n=4–5 is a typical, low-efficiency-loss subgroup size, and frames sampling frequency around Average Run Length — how fast a shift gets caught — rather than convenience. Useful directly for sizing a control chart on design-package defect rates or review throughput.

**[ISO's Auditing Practices Group publishes guidance on auditing design and development processes](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-Design%26Development.pdf)** — The practitioner paper structures clause 8.3 audits around planning, inputs, outputs, review/verification/validation, and change control, with a concrete list of objective evidence: planning docs, requirement traceability, review minutes, and change-control records. It's a ready-made structure for auditing your own team's design process, or checking a review file against before calling it complete.

**[ISO's Auditing Practices Group spells out what makes an internal audit program effective](https://committee.iso.org/files/live/sites/tc176/files/PDF%20APG%20New%20Disclaimer%2012-2023/ISO-TC%20176-TF_APG-InternalAudit.pdf)** — The joint ISO/IAF paper covers risk-based audit scheduling, auditor competence requirements, and the specific ways an audit's conclusions go wrong. It's a checklist for the shift from catching errors yourself to running a system that catches them, useful for designing or defending a design-review program to leadership.

**[NIST's handbook covers c-charts for tracking defect counts, with a real trap for design QC](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section3/pmc331.htm)** — The e-Handbook lays out the Poisson-based control-limit formula for counting defects, but flags that it assumes a fixed inspection-unit size. Design packages vary in size, so a straight c-chart will falsely flag big packages as out of control — normalize to defects per sheet or per route-mile before building a metric on it.
